What Is It Like to Be a Bat?

Thomas Nagel · 1974 · Consciousness and the limits of knowing

What if knowing everything still isn't understanding?

Imagine knowing everything science can tell us about a bat: its anatomy, echolocation, neural activity, and behavior. Would you know what it feels like to be that bat?

Nagel asks whether a third-person account can fully capture the first-person character of experience. After Plato's cave and William James's essay on the limits of understanding other lives, this reading takes the question into the philosophy of consciousness.

Distinguish the claim: This is a philosophical argument about the subjective character of experience, not experimental evidence that neuroscience is incapable of explaining consciousness, and not proof of a spiritual explanation.

Field Journal question: If we could completely explain how a mind works but couldn't experience what being that mind feels like, would we truly understand consciousness?

Productive tension: Could this gap reflect a limitation of our imagination rather than a permanent limitation of scientific knowledge?
